## What was checked, not assumed

The allocation column is a lookup and says nothing about what is transmitting
(ADR-0015). Three entries were measured with `make probe-periodicity`, which
tells LTE from NR and names the subcarrier grid without demodulating anything.

- **LTE band 28 is carrying 5G NR at 30 kHz**, not LTE. Six tunings across
  27 MHz of the downlink: a burst every 20 ms at 4 to 10 times its floor,
  nothing above 1.9 at 5 ms, and a cyclic prefix at 64 samples every time. The
  synchronisation block spans 3.81 MHz and this receiver reaches about
  2.4 MS/s, so it cannot be decoded here at all
  (`.scratch/nr-cell-search/`).
- **Band III is not DAB.** 17 candidates and a strong one at 179.976 MHz, so
  DAB+ looked like the obvious next decoder -- 1.536 MHz fits comfortably and
  the OFDM machinery would have been reused. Folding at DAB's 96 ms frame,
  where the phase reference symbol must repeat identically, gives a ratio of
  1.4. There is no DAB here. What is there is unidentified.
- **FM carries RDS.** 19 kHz pilot at 32 dB over the noise, and the 57 kHz
  band 10 dB over it. Measured as a band rather than a tone, because both the
  stereo and RDS subcarriers are suppressed and a measurement at the frequency
  itself finds nothing by design.

## Two things worth acting on

**88 candidates fall in gaps in the band plan** -- a third of the sweep, and
they include 416.652 MHz at −23.9 dBFS, the strongest thing here that is not
broadcast radio. There is a cluster of 16 across 400-500 MHz and another of 27
across 200-300 MHz. The band plan's job is to name what a frequency is
allocated to, and it is silent on the loudest non-broadcast signal in the
sweep (`.scratch/band-plan-coverage/`).

**FM broadcast is the strongest thing on the air by 14 dB** and it carries a
payload this program could read. See `.scratch/rds-decoder/`.

## Caveats on the levels

One antenna, one location, one afternoon, no LNA and default gain. Levels are
comparable *within* this sweep and to later sweeps taken the same way; they are
not absolute and they are not a statement about coverage. FM at −7.7 dBFS is
close enough to full scale that the front end may be compressing, which would
depress everything else in the sweep -- the ordering is safe, the gaps between
distant entries less so.
